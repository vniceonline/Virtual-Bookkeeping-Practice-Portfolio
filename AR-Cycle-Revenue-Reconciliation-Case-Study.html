Practice Case Study

End-to-End A/R Cycle & Revenue Reconciliation

Sample Company: Craig's Design and Landscaping Services  ·  QuickBooks Online Test Drive

Overdue Invoice Management Payment Processing Undeposited Funds & Deposits Bank Feed Matching Accrual vs. Cash Analysis Unbilled Revenue Audit

This exercise executed a complete Accounts Receivable cycle in QuickBooks Online — from identifying overdue invoices through payment collection, deposit batching, bank feed matching, and final financial statement analysis. The goal was to bring all open A/R balances to zero while verifying that every dollar collected flowed through cleanly to the company's books, with no duplicated income or unexplained variances.

Part 1Overdue Invoice Management & Payment Collection

Invoice Audit

Filtered the open invoice register to isolate past-due receivables, then issued a mix of batch and targeted invoice reminders to prompt client payment. This step surfaced all outstanding balances across the fiscal year rather than relying on ad-hoc follow-up.

Recording Payments

Recorded customer payments against specific open invoices — including multi-invoice customer accounts (e.g., Bill's Windsurf Shop, Freeman Sporting Goods) — allocating a single payment receipt correctly across multiple line items where needed. Across the full cycle, payments were received and applied against 9 open invoice records, clearing every past-due balance on the books.

Routing to Undeposited Funds

Rather than posting each payment directly to Checking, collected receipts were routed to Undeposited Funds — the correct intermediate step that mirrors how funds actually sit before a physical bank deposit is made. This keeps the audit trail clean and prevents income from posting to the bank register before it's actually deposited.

Result: Open invoice count moved from 9 items ($3,100.00) to 0 items ($0.00) — full A/R settlement, 100% collected.

Part 2Deposit Batching & Bank Feed Matching

Grouped Bank Deposits

Batched individual receipts sitting in Undeposited Funds into consolidated Bank Deposit transactions, matching each deposit total to what would physically hit the bank in a single transaction — first a $4,061.12 deposit across 12 payments, then a subsequent $8,973.74 batch clearing the remaining queue. Both were transferred out of Undeposited Funds and posted directly into the Checking account register.

Bank Feed Reconciliation

When the actual funds appeared in the bank feed, each deposit line was matched against its corresponding pre-recorded Bank Deposit — the correct sequence to avoid QuickBooks recording the same income twice (once from the invoice/payment, once again from the raw bank feed line). Remaining direct income and vendor purchase lines were categorized into their proper Chart of Accounts entries (Job Materials, Equipment Maintenance, Sales of Product Income), and every bank line was confirmed to have an assigned payee or customer for sub-ledger transparency.

Result: Zero uncollected invoice balance across the company dashboard, with all deposits reconciled against the bank feed and no duplicate income entries.

Part 3Unbilled Revenue Audit

A $750.00 Unbilled Income entry appeared on the business funnel dashboard, flagged for investigation rather than left unexplained. Tracing the underlying billable expense/time-tracking lifecycle — Billable Activity → Invoice → Payment → Deposit — confirmed the $750 had already been converted into Invoice #1007 (John Melton), fully collected, and properly reflected in total income.

Why This Matters

An "Unbilled Income" balance sitting on a dashboard can look like missing or leaking revenue if left unchecked. Rather than assuming it was an error — or ignoring it — the correct professional response was to trace it back through its full lifecycle and confirm it had already been properly billed and collected. That's the difference between reading a number and verifying it.

Strategic Insight

A $3,022.25 gap between Accrual and Cash Gross Profit shows the business maintains strong sales velocity on payment terms — invoices are being written and earned — but a meaningful share of that revenue was still sitting in Accounts Receivable rather than in the bank. On paper the business looks highly profitable; in practice, cash flow needs active monitoring so operating expenses can still be covered while collections catch up.

Key Takeaways

  • Executed a full Accounts Receivable cycle end-to-end — invoice audit, payment collection, deposit batching, and bank feed matching — clearing 9 open invoices worth $3,100.00 down to $0.00
  • Prevented duplicate revenue entries by routing every payment through Undeposited Funds before matching to the bank feed
  • Investigated a $750 Unbilled Income flag rather than dismissing or assuming it, tracing it to a fully collected invoice
  • Compared Accrual vs. Cash basis reporting to surface a real cash-flow insight beyond what the raw P&L shows on its face
  • Reinforced that financial reporting isn't just running a report — it's interpreting what the numbers mean for the business
